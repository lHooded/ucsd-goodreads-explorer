# Publication-year-aware canon pilot

## Design

This experiment adds one explicit bias to the ratings tuples: readers who give five stars to older works are treated as more literary. A work's primary year is the earlier of the modal BrightData `first_published` year and the minimum UCSD edition year. Oldness is zero at 2000 and rises linearly to one at 1850. A conservative reader score deducts uncertainty, and the primary top/bottom 10% cohorts are equal-sized. The ranking then compares balanced five-star versus one-to-three-star evidence in the old-reading jury with the same evidence in the low-old-reading jury; four-star ratings are neutral.

Book scoring is cross-fit by work-ID parity: the target book is always scored by a jury learned entirely from other books. A book's year does not directly enter its book score. Titles, authors, genres, and literary/anti lists were loaded only after all scores, stability tests, and sensitivity paths were frozen.

Year coverage: **26,403/26,418** candidate works, including **24,990** with an exact-joined BrightData year; runtime: **84.2s**.

Across the full joined catalogue, BrightData supplies an earlier date for **186,119** works. UCSD supplies the earlier logically possible date for **8,160** works.

Selected cleaned dates (best / BrightData / UCSD edition minimum): *Moby-Dick or, The Whale* 1851 / 1851 / 1923; *One Hundred Years of Solitude* 1967 / 1967 / 1969; *The Brothers Karamazov* 1880 / 1880 / 1912; *The Great Gatsby* 1925 / 1925 / 1925; *Ulysses* 1922 / 1922 / 1934.

## Findings

- The primary head is recognizably canonical: 11 exact seed-list works and 21 broader seed-author works in the top 50, with no anti-list books. This is a large change from the ratings-only methods, but it visibly favors school classics, children's classics, and classic fantasy.
- The smooth definition is not date-tuned: moving the full-oldness endpoint from 1850 to 1800 or 1900 retains 88.7% and 88.7% of the top-200 union respectively.
- The enriched year ranking versus the UCSD-only reconstruction has top-200 Jaccard **0.569**; this measures how much the repaired dates actually change the result.
- Rewarding volume as well as share selects jurors with more old-book evidence and keeps 58.7% of the top-50 union. Its fold-jury Jaccard is 0.615, versus 0.539 for the primary share score.
- The within-reader preference jury—users who rate older books higher than their own newer books—has fold-jury Jaccard **0.310** and primary top-200 Jaccard **0.208**, but disjoint-reader score rho **0.952**. It has 29 broad-literary works in the top 50 and **zero anti-list works through rank 500**.
- The preference path directly recovers several earlier omissions: *Moby-Dick* #22, *Middlemarch* #29, *Don Quixote* #44, *Ulysses* #126, and *The Great Gatsby* #174.
- The more literal old-minus-new mean-rating definition is stronger again: fold-jury Jaccard **0.394**, half-reader J@200 **0.743**, 11 exact and 31 broad literary works in the top 50, and zero anti-list works through rank 200. This is the cleanest current default for the requested old-over-new estimand; the correlation path remains a useful robustness check.
- Comparing the old-reading jury with all eligible readers retains only 7.5% of the primary top-50 union and has half-sample J@200 0.316. The matched low-old-reading contrast is therefore essential.
- A hard pre-1950 definition reaches deeper Russian and modernist works and suppresses genre books farther down, but it is much less stable and is a substantively different bias rather than a harmless tuning change.
